Life Admin Agent

One personal agent, built on verified identity, that handles the paperwork in people's lives: moving, renewals, claims and taxes. Taxes are year-round planning, not a once-a-year form.

Business goal

Become the one place a person goes when life creates paperwork. KYC is what makes this possible. It lets the agent act on the user's behalf, not just remind them. Taxes are the anchor: every person has to file each year, and every other module feeds the tax return with data.

WedgeMoveClear, one-off job. Partners pay referral fees. Builds the user's account map.
RetentionRenew + TaxRecurring reasons to come back: renewals any time of year, and filing every spring.
MoatLife contextThe agent already knows about the move, the baby and the claim before tax season, which TurboTax never does.

How the agent works

Every module runs the same loop. A trigger, whether a life event, a deadline or a document, starts a plan. Each step is sorted by who can do it, and the user approves anything that commits them.

Learning from Intuit

TurboTax shows that people will pay to avoid tax forms. Its weak spots are trust and timing, and those are where this product can be better.

Copy

  • Interview, not forms. Plain questions that become form lines without the user seeing them.
  • Imports. Photo of the W-2, employer and brokerage imports, last year's data carried forward.
  • Live refund meter. The number updates as you answer, which keeps people going.
  • Final accuracy review before filing, plus audit support.
  • Experts on demand. A CPA or EA can review or take over (TurboTax Live).

Do differently

  • Seasonal → year-round. TurboTax shows up after the year has closed, when most strategies can no longer be used.
  • Upsell maze → one clear price. Intuit paid a $141M multistate settlement in 2022 over how it advertised "free." Don't price by form complexity or upgrade people mid-flow.
  • Asks everything → infers first. Life context from other modules means fewer questions.
  • Finds deductions → explains choices. Show scenarios and why, so the user learns and decides.
  • Data for marketing → data for you. Tax data never goes to cross-sell partners.

Guardrails and compliance

  • E-filing. Filing returns electronically requires IRS authorized e-file provider status (EFIN), or a partnership with a provider that has it. This affects the build plan.
  • Paid preparers. If you charge to prepare returns, the CPAs or EAs who review them need PTINs and must follow preparer rules. Decide early whether the product is software the user files themselves, or a preparation service.
  • The user always signs. The agent never files, pays or commits money without explicit approval for that specific action.
  • Explain, then recommend. Every strategy shows its source rule, the dollar effect and the risk level. No positions that are unlikely to hold up in an audit.
  • Data. Tax data is used only for tax preparation unless the user consents (the IRC §7216 consent rules). Encryption, a SOC 2 audit and short retention for ID images.
  • Human fallback. Every journey has a person who can take over when automation or the user gets stuck.

Check before building: e-file rules, preparer requirements and state data laws vary. Have a tax attorney review the product before the pilot.
